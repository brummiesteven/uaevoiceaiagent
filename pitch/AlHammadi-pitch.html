The gap

Dubai opened its data to the world. You still need a screen to read it.

In 2015 Dubai became the first city anywhere to legally mandate the sharing, use and reuse of its data. It worked. All of it now sits behind a search box, a filter, a table and a download button.

For most people that's friction. If you're blind, or navigating with a motor impairment, or you simply find a dense administrative page hard going — “open” and “reachable” are not the same word.

The fix

So you ring up and ask.

No app. No page. No form. English or العربية. An answer in a sentence or two, with the source named.

Real call · 1 of 3

Caller

I use a wheelchair and I'm moving to Dubai next month. What help can I get with getting around?

Line

There's quite a bit set up for wheelchair users here. You get free travel on the Metro and buses with a special nol card, wheelchair spaces on every carriage, low-floor buses, accessible taxis around the clock, and if you drive, a free parking permit plus Salik exemption on one vehicle.

Sourced from RTA and Parkin. It hands over the phone number so the caller can act on it.

Real call · 2 of 3

Caller

What will my electricity bill be if I use about 2500 units a month?

Line

At around 2500 units a month you'd be looking at roughly 750 dirhams total — that's DEWA's residential rate, and it climbs a bit more per unit the higher your usage goes.

DEWA's slabs are marginal, not flat. A naïve calculation says 950 — a 27% overstatement, delivered confidently into someone's ear.

Real call · 3 of 3

Caller

What's the average rent for a two bedroom in Dubai Marina?

Line

I don't have that exact figure to hand. What I can tell you is that the Land Department tracks property transaction and pricing data more broadly — want me to check what's available instead?

The most important answer in the demo. It doesn't have it, so it says so. It does not guess.

Language

Dubai's data ships bilingual. The line doesn't have to stop there.

Every record in the catalogue carries both English and Arabic at source — title, description, theme, publisher. We didn't translate anything. The city already did.

Which means the language of the answer is independent of the language of the data. Speech in, speech out, in whatever language the caller actually speaks.

Most people living in Dubai speak neither English nor Arabic at home. A line that only works in two languages isn't a line for everyone.

Why it can be trusted

A wrong number is worse than no number.

Early on, with no tools attached, we asked it how many people live in Dubai. It answered “three million six hundred and fifty-three thousand, as of Q3 2023” — entirely invented, delivered with total confidence.

That is the failure this is built against. Every figure it speaks must come from a tool result in that conversation. It has no trusted memory of its own.

What we built on

Three tools, three jobs.

ElevenLabs
The interface

Speech in, the decision about what to look up, speech out — and the MCP connection to the data. Remove it and there is no call, and no way for the caller to reach any of this.

context.dev
The data no API has

DEWA's tariff table, RTA's entitlements, Parkin's permits, Dubai's headline statistics — all published only as rendered pages. It also got us past the portal's firewall by routing through a UAE address.

Devin
The support loop

A caller who isn't helped can say so, by voice. That becomes a ticket, and Devin is first responder — working what it can and flagging a human when it can't.

The vision it serves

Three commitments Dubai already made.

Nov 2013
My Community… A City for Everyone

HH Sheikh Hamdan bin Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum — to make Dubai a city that includes People of Determination. Dubai Law No. 2 of 2014 followed, protecting their rights.

Oct 2015
Dubai Data Law No. 26 of 2015

Issued by HH Sheikh Hamdan — making Dubai the first city in the world to mandate the sharing, use and reuse of its data. The reason 598 datasets exist to answer from.

Apr 2017
People of Determination

HH Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum renamed people with disabilities in recognition of what they achieve. The National Policy followed the same year.

Open data exists because of 2015.
The promise to reach everyone exists because of 2013 and 2017.

A voice line is simply what connects them.

Honestly

What's real — and what isn't yet.

  • Row-level data is gated.387 datasets publish an API, but it returns 401 without a key granted by email. The line explains how to request access rather than pretending it has the rows.
  • Tariffs and entitlements are a point-in-time capture.They change. The line says “worth confirming with them” and gives the number — but it needs a refresh job before real use.
  • Arabic is instructed, not yet verified.The data is bilingual and the agent is told to answer in Arabic. Nobody has yet sat through a full Arabic conversation end to end.
  • It has not been tested with People of Determination.Built for them, with care about language and dignity, checked against official sources — but not yet validated by the people it's for. That's the first thing to fix.
